Parse, validate, and project a Unix-style cron expression — list its next N fire times.

Use this when you are creating or auditing a scheduled job (sg-scheduler, OS cron, GitHub Actions, etc.) and you want to confirm the schedule means what you think it means before saving it. Pairs with the sg-scheduler MCP: validate the expression here first, then pass it to schedule_create.

Parameters:
- `expression` (required): a 5-field cron expression: `minute hour day-of-month month day-of-week`. Supports `*`, ranges (`1-5`), lists (`1,3,5`), step (`*/15`, `1-30/5`), aliases for month names (`jan`, `feb`, …) and weekday names (`mon`, `tue`, …, `sun`/`sun`=0 or 7), and the convenience macros `@yearly`/`@annually`, `@monthly`, `@weekly`, `@daily`/`@midnight`, `@hourly`. Day-of-week and day-of-month follow Vixie cron OR semantics (a fire happens if EITHER matches, when both are restricted).
- `count` (optional): how many upcoming fire times to compute. Range 1–50. Default 5.
- `from` (optional): ISO-8601 anchor for the projection (e.g. `2026-04-29T12:00:00Z` or `2026-04-29T12:00:00+02:00`). Defaults to "now". Useful for testing what a schedule would have done at a past or future point.
- `timezone` (optional): IANA timezone (e.g. `Europe/London`, `America/New_York`, `UTC`). Default is the host's local timezone. Cron expressions are interpreted in this timezone — so `0 9 * * *` in `Europe/London` always fires at 9 AM London time, including across DST transitions.

Behavior:
- Returns `metadata.valid` (bool), `metadata.normalized` (canonical form with macro/aliases expanded), and `metadata.next` (ISO-8601 strings, in the requested timezone).
- On invalid input, returns successfully with `valid: false` and a parse error in `metadata.error` instead of throwing — so an agent can branch on the result.
- The tool is pure — no network, no filesystem.

Examples:
- Every 15 minutes: `{ "expression": "*/15 * * * *" }` — returns the next 5 fire times.
- Daily 9 AM London: `{ "expression": "0 9 * * *", "timezone": "Europe/London", "count": 10 }`.
- Weekday business hours: `{ "expression": "0 9-17 * * mon-fri", "count": 8 }`.
- Macro: `{ "expression": "@hourly" }` — equivalent to `0 * * * *`.
- Audit a past schedule: `{ "expression": "5 4 * * sun", "from": "2026-01-01T00:00:00Z", "count": 6 }`.

Do NOT use this tool to:
- Run the cron job. This only computes next-fire times. Use sg-scheduler / OS cron / a daemon for actual execution.
- Parse 6-field (with seconds) or Quartz-style cron — this is the standard Unix 5-field form only.
